Case study

Dishes N'More

Over EGP 3 million in sales. A 15:1 average return on ad spend.

> EGP 3MEngagement sales
15:1Average ad ROAS
15 videosKitchen-campaign videos

Documented timing
Evidence window: site reports dated December 2024 to July 2025. The dinner-set production account specifies a one-day shoot. The reporting period for the EGP 3M sales total and average ROAS is not specified.

A brand and performance-marketing engagement for an Egyptian premium tableware business. The work combined a new visual direction, seasonal campaign production, an ecommerce website and conversion advertising. Beyond the headline sales result, dated site reports show 600 purchases in February 2025, compared with 9 in December 2024.

01

The starting point: a premium range selling on price

Dishes N'More had the products, but not yet a brand presentation that explained their value. The starting account describes roughly EGP 12,000 in online sales, with offers and discounts reaching 50%. The assignment was to establish the brand in Egypt and grow its ecommerce business. That meant working on the reason to buy, as well as the advertising used to bring buyers in.

02

Build a visual case for the price

The identity refresh and photography direction gave the collections a consistent premium presentation. For Christmas, that direction became a complete seasonal campaign, from styled glassware to banners and social posts. Rather than making the offer the only message, the creative work gave the products their own setting, materials and occasion.

03

Produce the assets around how people use the products

For the kitchen campaign, a setting was selected to match the brand, with models, photographers and production operations coordinated around the kitchenware range. The production delivered 15 promotional videos, product photography and lifestyle images. This supplied different ways to explain the same products: what they looked like, how they were used and how they fitted into a kitchen.

View image ↗View image ↗

04

Make each collection specific, without losing the brand

The creative direction extended into Ramadan cookware and dinner sets. The dinner-set production covered 15 sets in a one-day shoot. Close-ups showed texture and engraving; smaller food portions left the plate design visible; lifestyle scenes showed the products in use; clean set photographs explained the pieces included. These were distinct production choices for distinct buying questions, not interchangeable product posts.

05

Carry the product story into the ecommerce destination

The website brought the brand and its collections into an online storefront. Campaign creative, the product range and the ecommerce destination were developed within the same engagement, alongside search and performance work. The paid campaign evidence uses conversion objectives, so the commercial assessment went beyond attention and messages to recorded purchase value and ROAS.

View image ↗

06

Track the change in buying activity

The December 2024 site report records 644 active users and 9 purchases. By February 2025, the corresponding report records 24,000 active users and 600 purchases. These are dated site-wide analytics snapshots, not an attribution of every purchase to SEO. The project also contains later reports through July 2025, showing that the evidence extends beyond a single launch screenshot.

View image ↗View image ↗

07

Separate the commercial headline from individual ad results

The overall project account reports sales above EGP 3 million and average ROAS of 15:1. Separate conversion-campaign snapshots record EGP 782,558 in payment completion value at 16.34 ROAS, and EGP 278,259 at 14.21 ROAS. Each is presented on its own terms. Together, the evidence documents a wider commercial change supported by branding, production, the website and conversion campaigns, without treating a single creative asset as the sole cause.
