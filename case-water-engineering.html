Case file 01 · Water Engineering Consultations

Water Engineering Consultations

More than 600% quarterly revenue growth. From one office to four within the first year.1

A B2B market-entry programme across Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Oman and Libya, built on engineering credibility and qualified contacts rather than reach.2

  1. Stage 01 · The market

    The firm began with a single Egyptian office. I assessed the engineering-consultancy market in Egypt, then the opportunities in Saudi Arabia, Oman and Libya, benchmarking competitors with measurable growth. The resulting manpower, action and budget plans treated regional expansion as a market-entry problem, not an advertising one.

    • 1 → 4Offices in year one

    ResultFour markets, one plan. The channel mix included scientific conferences, contractor trade fairs, qualified outreach, LinkedIn, SMS and email rather than consumer-style social campaigns.3

  2. Stage 02 · The entry point

    Scientific conferences were selected as an entry point into the engineering community. Conference topics were shaped with the company’s consultants and engineering professors, while public relations maintained professional connections. Advertising around contractor trade fairs and engineering conferences drew attention to the firm’s national water-infrastructure track record.

    ResultThe message was grounded in relevant expertise and project experience, not in reach.4

  3. Stage 03 · Who to talk to

    Contact data for consultants and contractors was extracted and validated across all four markets. Qualification checked participation in national water-treatment tenders, the accuracy and relevance of contact details, and access to decision-makers rather than intermediaries.

    ResultOne database split in two, consultants and contractors, with separate email sequences and value propositions for each.5

  4. Stage 04 · How outreach ran

    LinkedIn campaigns broadened reach across the four markets, supported by the company’s project library and a dedicated team handling personalised outreach. The public relations manager oversaw external communication and monthly reporting on relationship-building outside Egypt.

    ResultCampaign activity stayed connected to ongoing professional contact, rather than depending on ad delivery alone.6

  5. Stage 05 · The email channel

    • 99.2%Email delivery
    • 28%Unique opens
    • 20%Unique clicks

    The email report records those three figures. The project account identifies email as a main contributor to improved international project turnover and profitability. That result sits behind the segmentation and qualification work: the programme was built to reach relevant organisations with a proposition matched to their role in the sector.

    ResultEmail was run as a commercial channel, not a mailing exercise.7

  6. Stage 06 · What it produced

    Channels went live in the first three months. After month three, resources were directed toward the most effective ones, and underperforming channels received sequential content strategies and a further three-month evaluation.

    ResultThe advertising/project report separately records 106 leads, 13 projects and revenue above EGP 91 million. The year-one outcome was a four-office regional presence and quarterly revenue growth above 600%; the source does not assign all of that revenue to email.8
