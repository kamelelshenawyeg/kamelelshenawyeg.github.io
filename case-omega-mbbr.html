Case study

Omega MBBR

Two production lines running at full capacity for approximately one year.

Documented timing
The documented duration is approximately one year of full-capacity production. Exhibition dates and the total marketing-engagement duration are not supplied.

Omega MBBR's marketing, exhibition and media programme helped turn attention into sustained demand for its water-treatment media. The factory attracted interest from Egyptian and Arab government audiences, while both production lines operated at full capacity for approximately a year. The assignment was to make a locally manufactured infrastructure product credible to a market accustomed to looking overseas for the technology.

01

The barrier was confidence in local manufacturing

Omega manufactures plastic media used in water-treatment systems, increasing the surface available for beneficial bacteria. The commercial challenge was a perception that this kind of engineering technology belonged to East Asia or America. The marketing plan addressed public-sector, government and commercial audiences in Egypt and the Gulf, with the product's quality, manufacturing capability and water-treatment role at the centre of the communication.

02

Make the factory part of the product story

Manufacturing media showed the production operation behind the technology, while product and social content explained what Omega supplied. The factory was not simply a company logo attached to an exhibition stand. Its manufacturing capability was part of the evidence presented to a market that needed confidence in a local supplier.

03

Bring event organisation and publicity into the same plan

I supported the company's first major appearance at the Water Expo in Egypt. Exhibition organisation, targeted advertising, promotional activity and press coverage were coordinated around that appearance. The stand created a place for direct sector contact; press and social coverage carried the story beyond those attending in person. This brought the product, the factory and the exhibition into one market-facing programme.

04

Convert visibility into a commercial opening

The exhibition and its media coverage attracted interest and conversations with Egyptian and Arab government audiences. Alongside targeted promotion, that attention contributed to demand for the factory's product. Both production lines operated at full capacity for approximately one year. For an infrastructure manufacturer, the value of the programme was not only visibility at an event, but sustained demand for what the factory could produce.

05

Support the same technical message across channels

The supporting social designs kept the product and its water-treatment application recognisable across the communication. Manufacturing content, exhibition material and press coverage served different purposes, but all presented the same local capability to the infrastructure market.
